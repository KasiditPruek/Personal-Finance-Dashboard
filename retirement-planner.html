How this is calculated

  • Each year until you retire, your salary grows by your salary growth rate. You and your employer add your PVD percentages of it to the provident fund, which earns the PVD return. Your monthly investing grows with your salary and earns its own return.
  • At retirement both pots combine and earn the after-retirement return. Each year, essentials, general spending, learning, and planned trips are taken out (in today's baht, raised by inflation) less any Social Security pension.
  • The Social Security pension needs 180 months of contributions. It pays 20% of your average wage over the last 60 months, with each month capped at that year's ceiling, plus 1.5% for each extra 12 months. It starts at 55, or when you retire if that's later, and is a fixed baht amount. Contributions are assumed to stop when you retire. Below 180 months you get a lump sum instead, which isn't modeled here.
  • "Needed at retirement" is the pot that would last exactly until your plan-until age. "To close the gap" finds the extra monthly investing, or the spending level, that makes the money last.
  • Tax, PVD vesting rules and fees aren't modeled. Returns are steady averages, not market ups and downs. This is a planning tool, not financial advice.
